#+TITLE: Task prioritization and capacity
#+STARTUP: overview

* Priority rules
- Keep the priority pyramid deliberately scarce across the active list:
  - A: about four immediate must-win tasks.
  - B: about seven important tasks, after A.
  - C: about nine useful tasks, after B.
  - Leave the remaining tasks without a priority rating.
- Anything actionable involving Nikita Mishin takes precedence within A.
- Money, people waiting, and tasks blocking other work qualify for consideration
  as A or B; they do not automatically receive A.
- Research, design, planning, administration, household work, hobbies, and
  speculative improvements receive a priority only when they genuinely compete
  for near-term attention.
- Rebalance the pyramid when an A task is completed or circumstances change.
- Give an avoided task a one-level boost if it is still worth doing.
- Consciously review any task that has gone 14 days without scheduling or review.

* Capacity baseline
- Weekdays contain eight working hours.
- Reserve five hours for active and consequential work.
- Protect three hours for research, deep thinking, and design on at least three
  weekdays.
- Begin with one 30--45 minute small-task batch.
- Choose one must-finish task and no more than two secondary tasks each day.
- On weekends, use no more than two structured hours on one day. Keep the other
  day mostly free and give personal tasks preference.

* Deferred-state rules
- WAIT records what or whom is being waited for, since when, and a follow-up date.
- HOLD records the reason, activation condition, and review date.
- Review FUTURE monthly and activate at most one or two items per review.
- Repeatedly skipped FUTURE items must be reaffirmed, rewritten, or removed.
